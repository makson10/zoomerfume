## Overview

Sauvage Eau de Toilette is the original Sauvage, launched by Dior in 2015 and composed by François
Demachy. It's one of the most worn men's scents of the last ten years: bright citrus, a peppery bite
and a dry, mineral base. It's the freshest and sharpest of the three Sauvage versions we carry, next
to the Eau de Parfum (KB-002) and the Elixir (KB-003). Don't confuse it with Eau Sauvage, a much
older Dior fragrance from 1966 that smells completely different and isn't in our catalog.

## Notes

Dior names only three key notes for the Eau de Toilette and gives no full pyramid:

- **Top:** Calabrian bergamot
- **Heart:** Sichuan pepper
- **Base:** ambroxan

Longer lists you may see elsewhere also mention lavender, pink pepper, geranium, vetiver, patchouli,
elemi, cedar and labdanum.

## How it wears

It opens loud and bright: juicy bergamot with a pepper tingle that feels almost fizzy. The pepper
stays for a while, then the scent dries down to clean, slightly salty ambroxan that's easy to
recognize from across a room. On most of our team it lasted 7–9 hours. It projects strongly for the
first few hours and then settles into a clear trail. Next to the Eau de Parfum it feels sharper and
fresher, because there's no vanilla in the base.

## Who it's for

- Someone who wants a fresh, confident everyday scent that's very hard to get wrong.
- Spring to autumn: weekends, going out and the office, where one or two sprays are enough.
- A safe gift for a man whose taste you don't know well.

Who it's not for: anyone tired of smelling Sauvage on every second person, or someone who finds
peppery freshness too sharp. For a softer, quieter fresh scent, look at Jo Malone London Wood Sage &
Sea Salt Cologne (KB-024).

## Zoomer's take

The OG Sauvage, and yes, half the city wears it. That's not a red flag: it's fresh, clean and gets
compliments for a reason. Stuck between the EDT and the EDP? Grab a sample of each. The EDT wins if
you want more sparkle and plan to wear it all summer.
